My Thesis

I wrote a Python bot that trades Kalshi’s KXBTCD markets: hourly “Bitcoin above $X” contracts spaced roughly $100 apart. The idea is simple — buy the floor as NO and the ceiling as YES. If price leaves that band by hour’s end, one leg pays $1. If it stays inside, both expire worthless.

This page walks through the strategy, an 800-day Binance backtest, a short scored trade book from Notion, how the live loop works, and a handful of real order attempts from the bot log.

Stack: Python, Kalshi REST API, Binance 1m klines, Notion for the paper book.

Strategy

The bet is that Bitcoin will not finish the hour inside one $100 band — not that it goes up.

The bot locks the band that held the previous hour’s close. It buys NO on the floor and YES on the ceiling.

NO pays if the hour finishes at or below the floor. YES pays if it finishes above the ceiling. The gap between them is the loss case.

btcprice.py measures that exit on 1.15M one-minute BTCUSDT candles. strategy.py stores hour-of-day exit rates from that run. Live, the bot only trades when the stored rate is ≥79%, the two legs cost ≤65¢ combined, and each leg clears a 5¢ edge.
